I am a PhD student in the Department of Engineering at the University of Cambridge and a member of Trinity Hall, supervised by Prof. Albert Guillén i Fàbregas. I am part of the Probabilistic Systems, Information, and Inference (PSI2) Group in the Division of Information Engineering, and my PhD is generously supported by the Cambridge Trust. Before Cambridge, I studied Computer Engineering and Applied Mathematics at Sharif University of Technology.

My research focuses on error exponents in channel and joint source-channel coding. I derive achievability and converse bounds under mismatched and universal decoding, using tools from large deviations and probabilistic combinatorics. More broadly, I am interested in information theory, applied probability, and combinatorics.

selected publications

  1. Submitted
    The Expurgated Error Exponent is Not Universally Achievable
    AmirPouya Moeini, Marco Dalai, and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas
    Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2026
    Abs arXiv

    We study the universal attainability of the expurgated error exponent for discrete memoryless channels (DMCs). While the random-coding exponent is known to be universally attainable via maximum mutual information (MMI) decoding for DMCs, it remains open whether the expurgated exponent can be attained universally. We show that this is not the case in general. Specifically, we construct a family of DMCs for which no single sequence of codes can attain the expurgated exponent simultaneously for all channels in the family, even at rate zero. In addition, for the same channel family, we show that MMI decoding fails to achieve the expurgated exponent for any channel in the family.

  2. Submitted
    Universal Decoding for Unequal Error Protection
    AmirPouya Moeini and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas
    Submitted to IEEE Information Theory Workshop (ITW 2027), 2026
    Abs arXiv

    We study universal unequal error protection of a single special message over discrete memoryless channels. We construct a sequence of codes with a decoding rule that does not depend on the channel transition law. We derive an achievable error exponent for the special message while ensuring reliable decoding of the ordinary messages. The resulting exponent strictly improves on the natural equal-error-protection random-coding baseline and remains positive as the rate approaches capacity. For symmetric channels, it converges to the optimal red-alert exponent at capacity.

  3. Submitted
    An Upper Bound to the Undetected Error Exponent with Erasure Decoding at Positive Rates
    AmirPouya Moeini, Marco Dalai, and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas
    Submitted to IEEE Information Theory Workshop (ITW 2027), 2026
    Abs arXiv

    We study the undetected-error exponent of discrete memoryless channels with erasure decoding. Assuming that the undetected-error and erasure probabilities vanish, we derive an upper bound on the undetected-error exponent at positive rates. The bound uses Somekh-Baruch’s genie-aided argument where an auxiliary output provides the decoder with a list of codewords. It recovers Telatar’s tight rate-zero converse and is at least as tight as the straight-line bound. A numerical example shows that the improvement over the straight-line bound can be strict.

  4. ISIT 2026
    Random Gilbert-Varshamov Codes for Joint Source-Channel Coding
    AmirPouya Moeini and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas
    In IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory (ISIT), 2026
    Abs DOI arXiv

    We propose a random coding technique for joint source-channel coding of discrete memoryless sources and channels. The approach builds on the random Gilbert-Varshamov code construction of Somekh-Baruch et al. and extends it to the joint source-channel setting. We show that the resulting ensemble attains the maximum of the random-coding and expurgated error exponents.
